CyberDolls — Digital Identity & Visual Distortion

A real-time generative art project that merges AI image synthesis with post-processing systems built in TouchDesigner. Anime-style figures generated through AI are subjected to dithering, displacement mapping, and layered pixel effects — fragmenting the source image into something unstable and synthetic.

The result questions the boundaries between digital identity, constructed beauty, and visual corruption. Each output is unique, generated in real-time, and never fully resolved.

03NARRATIVE

Concept. CyberDolls is a series about digital identity, constructed beauty and visual corruption. Each face is built to be desirable and then deliberately broken: halftone dots eat the skin, outlines wobble and tear, and the image keeps sliding between portrait and noise. Every output is unique. It is generated in real time and never finishes resolving, so the viewer is always watching a face in the middle of becoming, or of dissolving.

Pipeline. The process starts with AI image generation to create the characters. Those images are then dithered, pushed through displacement mapping, and covered with layered pixel effects built in TouchDesigner and GLSL. The algorithm is not a filter applied at the end: it is the second author of the piece. Because the effects run live, the same character never looks the same twice, and the glitch is a feature of the portrait rather than a flaw in it.

04ARTIST

Johann Jair Andrés Martínez, known as “Andro” and under the handle punk_bit, is a self-taught multimedia artist and creative technologist based in Colombia. His practice spans real-time generative art, GLSL shaders, TouchDesigner, video mapping, live VJing, holography on dichromated gelatin, LED installations and 3D work in Blender. Through the Volarte initiative, supported by CoCrea, he collaborated with museums and airports across Latin America, bringing animated artworks to large public LED screens. He created live visuals for Bring Me The Horizon concerts and projected onto a building facade at OFFF Festival Barcelona 2025. Across formats, his work treats code as a visual language and art as an interface for opening new aesthetic possibilities.
